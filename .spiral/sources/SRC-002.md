# Source: Semantic Continuity Feasibility Cycle Direction

## Source Kind

Connected conversation.

## What Was Actually Expressed Or Observed

The maintainer described a research cycle about semantic continuity across SLO
curriculum dataset evolution.

SLO curriculum datasets are intended to let third parties retain durable
references to entities by following `replacedBy` relations. This works when an
entity is directly edited or an existing dataset is modified in place. It is
much harder when a later implementation of substantially the same curriculum
material was created independently: identifiers and names may have changed,
concepts may have moved in the hierarchy, concepts may have split or merged,
scope may have broadened or narrowed, and concepts may have been redistributed
across parents.

Historically, establishing these continuity relations required substantial
domain-expert work and was often not done.

The stated research question is whether current AI can make trustworthy semantic
continuity mapping between such dataset versions cheap enough that maintaining
these links becomes practically feasible.

The maintainer emphasized:

- there is no known successful historical split/merge migration available as
  ground truth;
- there are different implementations of substantially the same kinds of
  datasets, produced at different times with evolved modelling insights and
  naming;
- the outcome may be positive, negative, or conditional;
- AI must not be assumed to be an appropriate solution;
- this cycle is independent of production integration;
- the Open Data maintenance architecture must not be modified merely to conduct
  the experiment;
- the cycle should produce enough evidence to decide whether semantic-continuity
  assistance deserves to become a first-class capability in the
  maintainer-workspace roadmap.
